Methodology (the serious part)

The state rankings use the original listed price of the cheapest standard nasi lemak ayam (fried or spiced chicken variant) and the basic ayam gepuk set on public delivery-platform menus, collected by hand, dated, and published with sources. The T-20 Menu uses the highest-priced nasi lemak dish of any kind we could find on the same platforms. Delivery prices run above counter prices; we use one consistent basis so states compare fairly. Target sample: 10 restaurants per dish per state. Perlis and Putrajaya nasi lemak counts sit below 10 because we counted every listed vendor there. All of them. No prices are invented, estimated, or vibed.

Fun methodology fact: the "most expensive state" title changed hands four times as the sample grew. Small samples are confident liars, which is exactly why we kept counting to 10.

The Share Eat! manifesto

Discovery Shouldn't Be Expensive.

Malaysia has thousands of great restaurants. But being great doesn't guarantee being discovered.

At the same time, food creators are expected to constantly create, often spending their own money just to find the next restaurant, shoot the next video and keep their audience fed with something new.

We think there's a better way. Restaurants have something worth sharing. Creators have people worth sharing it with. Share Eat! brings them together.

And because we're obsessed with Malaysian food, we're starting by understanding it. That's why we built the Harga Index.

Because great food deserves to be discovered.
And discovery shouldn't be expensive.
