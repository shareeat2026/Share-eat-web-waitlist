Our story

Discovery shouldn't
be expensive.

Why a food creator platform ended up counting the price of nasi lemak in every state of Malaysia.

🍽️The restaurant problem

There are thousands of great restaurants in Malaysia. But being good doesn't guarantee being discovered.

A restaurant can have amazing food, fair prices and loyal customers, and still be invisible online. Meanwhile, restaurants with huge marketing budgets dominate everyone's feeds.

🎥The creator problem

At the same time, food creators are expected to constantly create. Find a restaurant, travel there, buy the food, shoot, edit, post. Repeat. And every piece of content costs them money.

One TikTok, out of pocket:

RM30 food + RM15 Grab + RM10 parking = RM55

Do that three or four times a week and you're spending RM600 to RM900 a month just to create content. For smaller creators, that's ridiculous.

🤝So we built Share Eat!

Restaurants have food that needs to be discovered. Creators have audiences that want to discover food. There's just too much friction between them.

🏪 Restaurants
discovery + content + customers
⇄
🎥 Creators
free food + opportunities + relationships

Restaurants list campaigns and pay creators in food, not cash. Creators dine, film, and keep every video. Consumers get more great restaurants to discover. That's the flywheel.

📊Then, the Index happened

We wanted to understand Malaysian food culture from the perspective of data, so we started going through restaurant menus. And we noticed something: there are thousands of restaurants on delivery platforms, but there isn't really a fun, accessible way for Malaysians to understand what food actually costs.

So we started collecting the data. Plate by plate. State by state. And we figured: if we're already collecting it, why not make it useful?

That's how the Harga Index was born. It isn't a marketing gimmick. It's a by-product of the mission: we're literally making food discovery more accessible through data.
